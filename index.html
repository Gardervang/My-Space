Why this exists

Army games almost all turn into administration. You stop fighting a battle and start processing one — rolling buckets of dice, ticking health bars down, removing models because the arithmetic said so. The cinema is in your head. Never in front of you.

I did not want a bigger spreadsheet.
I wanted a bigger feeling.

Every system in this studio is built on the same rule: scale the war, not the bookkeeping. The moment you learn to manufacture against a single operative in the skirmish — the enemy out of reactions, out of cover, one clean shot from the end — you now manufacture across a whole battlefield. While they try to do the same to you.

Good games should be reachable. The rules that make a battle worth playing don't belong behind a paywall — they belong on the table, dog-eared and dice-scarred. If this work earns a place on your table, a donation keeps the next manual coming.

RANGE 30″

The core loop

ACT

Action

One meaningful action per activation. Move freely, then act — or act, then move. Every decision costs something. That's what makes it a decision.

REA

Reaction

Spend it to dodge a hit, intercept a charge, or fire back. Lose it carelessly and the next attack lands unreacted. Your Reaction is your survival — guard it like ground.

EXP

Exposed

No cover. No Reactions. You are the target. The game is about manufacturing this state in your enemy — and never being in it yourself.

Learn it once, carry it everywhere. The same loop runs across WWII infantry, elite sci-fi kill-teams, and full army-scale battle. Sit down at any Wounds & Steel table and you already know how to play.
